Tables keep the wall buffer from solid walls and obstacles, never from each other. A chair needs its width and depth clear of walls, tables and other chairs.

The table space is the area a person uses for plates, papers or a laptop. Its width spaces the chairs when it is wider than a chair. Its depth is trimmed at the middle of the table so people can sit face to face. Spaces on adjoining edges never overlap, so seats move off a corner or drop out, whichever seats more.

Offset is measured from the left end of a top or bottom wall, or the top end of a side wall. Tables and chairs stay out of the clear zone.

Obstacles and no-go areas

An obstacle is solid, like a column or counter, and tables keep the wall buffer from it. A no-go area is open floor that tables and chairs must not cover. X and Y are measured from the top left corner of the room.
